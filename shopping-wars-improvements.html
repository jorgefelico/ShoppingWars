Recommendations

#1 · GAME FEEL

Add a combat-feedback layer

The single highest-ROI change: make every hit felt and readable. Low–Med effort · High impact

The problem — hits are neither felt nor readable

Every damage path was traced. When a player is hit, the victim gets only camera shake + a red screen flash — nothing happens in the world. The attacker gets a melee swing tween + a small trauma bump — no hit marker, no tick sound, no damage number. The splatter effects only fire for items hitting surfaces, never for player hits.

In a 240s Battle Royale constantly wrapped in DenseFog, LightsOut, and SpeedFrenzy, players can't tell if they connected, how much it did, or where it came from. Readability is the #1 gameplay-quality lever, and it's the biggest missing piece. A grep across all scripts for hit-marker / damage-number / blood / hit-sound returns zero matches.

What I'd build
  • Hit marker + tick on the attacker — flash the crosshair layer + a short tick sound.
  • Floating damage numbers above the victim, color-coded by amount.
  • Impact VFX at the hit point (a small blood/impact burst reusing the existing multi-emitter pattern), replicated so bystanders and spectators see it too.
  • Directional damage indicator — an edge vignette on the damage overlay pointing at the attacker; this is what makes damage readable during the fog/blackout events.
Where it hooks in (the events & RPCs already exist)
Health.RpcSyncHealth — already computes damageTaken on the client → single clean hook for damage numbers Health.Damaged event PlayerController.RpcOnMeleeSwing(hitPos, didHit) Product.RpcOnImpact PlayerController.OnPlayerDamaged DamageOverlay.Flash(damage)

All the hooks already exist — this is assembly, not architecture.

#2 · DESIGN / STRATEGY

Make the shopping phase a real strategic fight

The namesake phase is currently the least strategic part of the loop. Med effort · High impact

The problem — shopping resolves to "grab the 5 most expensive weapons"

In ProceduralShelfFiller.StockSurface, every shelf slot picks uniformly at random from the category pool — no rarity weighting, no per-type cap. A $30 / 50-damage PropaneTank is as likely to spawn as a $2 SodaCan. There's no designed scarcity, so there's no reason to scout during the 5s transition, no racing for a contested item, no risk.

Compounding it, the economy is dead during combat: the kill reward is AddMoney(50) + 35 HP + a 4s speed boost, but cash can only be spent in the Shopping phase, and Money resets every round. So the +$50 you earn mid-battle is unspendable — the economy has zero presence in the 240s of actual fighting.

What I'd build
  • Weighted rarity + per-type caps in the filler: add a Weight/Rarity field to ProductEntry, make flagship weapons genuinely rare (e.g. 1–2 sledgehammers, 2–3 propane tanks per store), and cap total spawns per product name.
  • Rotate stock per round — re-stock with a fresh seed on ResetForNewRound so the layout can't be memorized.
  • Make cash live in battle — let any player buy from shelves during Battle Royale at full price (not just the Scavenger perk), or spawn periodic restock/cash drops. Either way it connects the shopping economy to combat and makes the existing kill reward meaningful.
Where it hooks in
ProceduralShelfFiller.ProductEntry — add Weight / MaxCount ProceduralShelfFiller.StockSurface — weighted pick + per-name cap GameManager.ResetForNewRound — fresh stock seed Product.Interact — open purchase gate in BattleRoyale PlayerController.OnEliminatedEnemy — kill cash becomes usable

Bottom Line

  • #1 is the "feel" fix — cheap relative to impact, mostly additive VFX/RPC work on hooks that already exist. Do this first.
  • #2 is the "identity" fix — a focused change to ProceduralShelfFiller plus a small purchase-gate tweak in Product.Interact. This is what turns "grab weapons" into actual gameplay.
